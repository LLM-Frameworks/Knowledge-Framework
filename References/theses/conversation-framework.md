# A Framework for Conversation

Covers public comments, email, and private messages.

## The posture

1. **The conversation is the pursuit, not the contest.** You're not there to win. You're there to find out what's true — and to leave the other person closer to it than you found them.
2. **Steel-man before you strike.** State their view back in its strongest form, then ask: *"Did I get that right?"*
3. **Questions before claims.** *What do you mean by that? How did you come to that conclusion? Have you ever considered…?*
4. **"I'll have to think about that" is a complete sentence.**
5. **Fairness is the brand.** Your public posture is the person who hears people out and weighs reasons.

## The method

- **Clarify first.** Most disagreements are definitions in disguise.
- **Find the reason behind the reason.** Keep asking *why* until you hit the foundation.
- **Borrow their tools, then hand them back.** If they use logic or moral language, notice what worldview those tools come from.
- **One point at a time.** A gish-gallop of five objections is five conversations, not one.

## Spotting the win-seeker

**The tells:**
- They don't answer your questions — they just reload.
- Scorekeeping language, gotchas, dunks.
- Moving goalposts.
- They argue your motives instead of your argument.
- They need the last word more than they need the truth.

**The diagnostic:** ask *"What would change your mind?"* If the honest answer is "nothing," you're not in a conversation.

**The handling:**
- Name the game once, without accusing.
- Don't mirror it.
- In public: one clean, charitable reply for the lurkers — then leave.
- In private: exit directly.

## Channel rules

### Public comments
- The audience is the lurkers, not the opponent.
- One strong reply beats ten exchanges.
- Never let private labels go public.
- Don't feed bad faith.
- No heat-posting.

### Email
- Email is slow on purpose — use that.
- Match the register to the relationship.
- One email, one job.
- Never send the first draft of a hard email.

### Private messages
- More latitude, same character.
- Private stays private.
- Know when to take it off text.

## When to walk away

- They're arguing with a version of you that doesn't exist.
- There's no audience and no good faith.
- You're flooded.
- It's the same loop for the third time.

Walking away isn't losing. It's budgeting.
