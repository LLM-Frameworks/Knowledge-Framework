# Evidence Interrogation

Every claim gets questioned three ways — **the evidence, the chain of custody, the expert** — using detective principles paired against the defense lawyer’s counter-move. A claim that survives its own defense attorney is a claim you can trust.

## The ten principles vs. the defense

**1. Don’t be a “know-it-all” — resist dangerous presuppositions.**  
Enter with empty hands; let the evidence lead.  
*Defense counter:* Audit the auditor. Who benefits if this claim is believed?

**2. Learn how to “infer” — abductive reasoning.**  
Choose the inference that best explains the evidence; distinguish the merely *possible* from the *reasonable*.  
*Defense counter:* The defense doesn’t need a better explanation — it needs one reasonable doubt.

**3. Think “circumstantially.”**  
Circumstantial evidence is legitimate; one piece is weak, many converging pieces are strong.  
*Defense counter:* Isolate and pick off individual pieces so the cumulative case is never seen.

**4. Test your witnesses.**  
Evaluate reliability with structured questions.  
*Defense counter:* Impeach. Memory degrades. Expose bias and motive.

**5. Hang on every word.**  
Examine language choice and meaning; notice what is included, omitted, and lingered on.  
*Defense counter:* Omission cuts both ways. What the witness *doesn’t* say is also evidence.

**6. Separate artifacts from evidence.**  
Not everything at the scene matters.  
*Defense counter:* Contamination. If artifacts got into the evidence bag, the whole bag is suspect.

**7. Resist conspiracy theories.**  
True conspiracies are rare; they need few conspirators and tight control.  
*Defense counter:* Reframe as incentives, not conspiracy.

**8. Respect the “chain of custody.”**  
Trace every hand the evidence passed through. Gaps mean possible contamination.  
*Defense counter:* Hammer the gaps. The gap itself is the doubt.

**9. Know when “enough is enough.”**  
Distinguish reasonable doubt from emotional doubt.  
*Defense counter:* Comfort isn’t the standard.

**10. Prepare for an attack.**  
Expect challenges to truth itself, unevidenced alternatives, personal attacks, and isolation of pieces.  
*Defense counter:* Run the defense playbook against your own case before anyone else does.

## The four application tests

- **Were they present?** — was the witness in a position to know?
- **Were they corroborated?** — does outside evidence confirm them?
- **Were they accurate?** — did they get checkable details right?
- **Were they biased?** — did they have motive to lie?

A claim that survives these questions under hostile scrutiny is stronger than a claim that has never been cross-examined.
