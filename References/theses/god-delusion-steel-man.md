# The God Delusion — chapter-by-chapter steel-man

Companion to the debate guide. Richard Dawkins’s *The God Delusion* (2006) at its strongest, with the best response paired to each chapter.

**Evidence status:** [Theory] for chapter reconstructions until verified against the primary text; secondary critical responses may support [Likely] on specific points once receipts are re-opened. Do not raise any tag above [Theory] on a chapter claim without checking the book.

## Ch 1 — “A Deeply Religious Non-Believer”

**Steel-man:** Distinguishes “Einsteinian religion” (quasi-mystical pantheism) from “supernatural religion.” Only the latter is the target. God’s existence is treated as a scientific hypothesis.

**Response:** Grant the distinction. But “scientific hypothesis” smuggles in scientism: most theists hold God as the best *explanation* of reality, not a lab result.

## Ch 2 — “The God Hypothesis”

**Steel-man:** Monotheism emerged from polytheism; the 7-point spectrum of theistic probability places Dawkins at 6 (“de facto atheist”).

**Response:** Origins don’t settle truth (genetic fallacy). The scripture handling is drive-by exegesis; grant the honest difficulty of hard OT passages.

## Ch 3 — “Arguments for God’s Existence”

**Steel-man:** Runs through Aquinas’s Five Ways, ontological argument, beauty, experience, scripture, Pascal’s wager — finds them vacuous against folk versions.

**Response:** Scholarly responses document systematic misrepresentation of the strongest forms (Hart, Ward, McGrath, Craig, Plantinga). Steel-man the actual arguments, not the folk versions.

## Ch 4 — “Why There Almost Certainly Is No God” (strongest chapter)

**Steel-man:** The Ultimate Boeing 747 gambit. A designer must be at least as complex as the designed; natural selection is the bottom-up alternative. Occam’s razor and God-of-the-gaps warning.

**Response:** Classical theism posits divine simplicity — God is not a complex object in the universe. Natural selection explains biological complexity, not cosmic fine-tuning. The multiverse multiplies entities.

## Ch 5 — “The Roots of Religion”

**Steel-man:** Faith as a by-product of useful cognitive mechanisms + memes.

**Response:** Genetic fallacy. Explaining *how* belief arises doesn’t settle *whether* it’s true. Self-undercut: if faculties are survival-shaped misfirings, why trust the theorizing faculties? (Plantinga’s evolutionary argument against naturalism.)

## Ch 6 — “The Roots of Morality”

**Steel-man:** Altruism has Darwinian roots. People would still behave morally without God.

**Response:** Explains *capacity*, not *obligation* (is/ought gap). The moral argument’s force is “what makes anything *binding*?”

## Ch 7 — “The ‘Good’ Book and the Changing Moral Zeitgeist”

**Steel-man:** Moral zeitgeist evolves; churches follow as often as lead. We pick and choose scripture.

**Response:** Grant moral progress and institutional failures. The zeitgeist itself needs explaining. Principled hermeneutics exist; arbitrary picking-and-choosing is not the only option.

## Ch 8 — “What’s Wrong with Religion?”

**Steel-man:** Religion subverts science, fosters fanaticism, encourages bigotry, functions as in-group/out-group label.

**Response:** Grant real evils done under religious banners. The charge proves too much (politics and nationalism also divide). Confuses believers’ behavior with beliefs’ truth. The 20th-century secular violence ledger must be included.

## Ch 9 — “Childhood, Abuse and the Escape from Religion”

**Steel-man:** Children shouldn’t be labeled by parents’ religion.

**Response:** Grant the label point. Distinguish teaching from indoctrination. Every worldview transmits itself to children. The question is whether what’s transmitted is true.

## Ch 10 — “A Much-Needed Gap?”

**Steel-man:** Consolation and inspiration aren’t evidence. Atheism can be life-affirming.

**Response:** Fair. Comfort doesn’t prove truth. The reverse is also true: discomfort doesn’t disprove it.
